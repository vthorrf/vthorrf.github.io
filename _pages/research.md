<p class="page-lede">My research focuses on the foundations and methods of psychological measurement. I am particularly interested in situations where conventional psychometric models are useful but their assumptions are stronger than the psychological theory warrants.</p>

## Research program

<div class="research-block">
  <span class="research-number">01</span>
  <div>
    <h3>Measurement theory and psychometrics</h3>
    <p>I study the connection between the mathematical structure of a psychometric model and the psychological claims that can legitimately be made from it. This includes fundamental and representational measurement, item response theory, nonparametric models, scale construction, and methods for identifying or relaxing common psychometric assumptions.</p>

Topics include hierarchical models, Monte Carlo algorithms, variational methods, Bayesian graphical models, nonparametric regression, and model comparison.</p>

## Current methodological directions

My current work includes the development of alternative item-response and optimal-scoring models, extensions of Bayesian computation for flexible regression and latent-variable models, and approaches that connect psychometric analysis more closely to theories of measurement and cognition. A recurring goal is to build methods that are **theoretically explicit, computationally usable, and empirically testable**.
